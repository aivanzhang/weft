  10+ years of building AI-native startups, investing in and advising AI-native companies, and consulting for Fortune 1,000 companies to help them move towards being AI-native, I finally feel like I can share the story that I've seen over and over again. The path for most companies incorporating AI into their offerings usually goes like: 1. Renting the closed frontier from companies like [OpenAI](https://www.linkedin.com/company/openai?trk=public_profile__posts-text), [Google](https://www.linkedin.com/company/google?trk=public_profile__posts-text), and [Anthropic](https://www.linkedin.com/company/anthropicresearch?trk=public_profile__posts-text) because they market themselves as being the best bang for your buck (spoiler alert, they aren't) 2. AI engineering your systems, harnesses, context retrieval, etc to squeeze the most you possibly can out of your closed models 3. Recognize that you can achieve a similar level of performance from your AI systems by routing to open models while saving on cost 4. Realize that at a certain point, all you're doing is waiting and hoping for the next best model and finally coming to the conclusion that the only way to achieve long term AI independence and success is to move your logic, skills, and voice into the weights of the model itself to own your specialized intelligence. [Harvey](https://www.linkedin.com/company/harvey-ai?trk=public_profile__posts-text) knows this. [Cursor](https://www.linkedin.com/company/cursorai?trk=public_profile__posts-text) knows this. [Cognition](https://www.linkedin.com/company/cognition-ai-labs?trk=public_profile__posts-text) knows this. The list goes on. The AI-native companies like them aren't following a different path than the AI-dependent, they just follow the path quicker and with conviction. They start by renting intelligence from a closed lab, engineering a stellar harness with context coming in at just the right time, and then eventually go on to try open models and then eventually train those same open models on their data, truly becoming AI-native. Where are you on this path to becoming AI-native? What will it take for you to move to the next step? Find out in the first part of my multi-part webinar series. Link in the first comment.   Something I wish I got asked a lot: "Sinan, what reasoning level should I use?" Most people just assume that cranking reasoning to 11 is always the way no matter what, and frankly I don't blame them given all the model marketing that comes out these days showing that, overall, more reasoning tends to yield better quality results. Well it doesn't always look like that when you drill down to a specific use-case and test set. Let's try running four different LLMs (1 open, 3 closed) on a computer use dataset across all of their provided reasoning efforts: - NO model did best while on their maximum reasoning effort - K3 and GPT 5.5 really overthink at their maximum reasoning efforts - Opus did the best at low reasoning effort Test reasoning efforts on your data, please!   If you're training LoRAs today or even just thinking about fine-tuning, this post is for you. When LoRA underperforms full parameter fine-tuning (FullFT), it's tempting to assume that LoRA adapters simply aren't strong enough. But the performance gap between LoRA and FullFT is often hard to attribute without checking. A new writeup from us at [Fireworks AI](https://www.linkedin.com/company/fireworks-ai?trk=public_profile__posts-text) lays out three things to check before you commit to FullFT: → Learning rate / the training recipe in general. LoRA usually wants a much higher LR than FullFT. In one of these runs the winning LR was 8e-5 vs 1e-6, an 80× difference. → Data coverage. On one task, 10× more unique data on the same step budget moved quality from 34% to 68% before any other changes were made. → Adapter rank. It helped on some tasks and barely moved others, so it's worth sweeping rather than assuming a single value. Before you reach for more expensive FullFT, consider your recipe, coverage, and LoRA rank and as always, feel free to ask me any questions. Happy Experimenting!
