  The LLM industry is (thankfully) moving past RAG, chatbots and report-generation; we are now excited about 🤖 Agents 🤖 - autonomous function-calling AI systems that can ideate, plan and execute without humans in the loop! The problem? Present day LLMs are still far from perfect. The same stochasticity that enables LLMs to compose ballads about static compilers ([https://lnkd.in/e_ABqBX9](https://lnkd.in/e_ABqBX9?trk=public_profile__posts-text)) can sometimes hallucinate or go down undesirable directions in latent space. This is more dangerous when these systems are able to operate and interact entirely devoid of human intervention. LLM Safety is an active and important area of research; at Meta we're building tools like Llama Guard ([https://lnkd.in/eYXRA22X](https://lnkd.in/eYXRA22X?trk=public_profile__posts-text)) and publish a Responsible Use Guide ([https://lnkd.in/er92wy-T](https://lnkd.in/er92wy-T?trk=public_profile__posts-text)) to minimize the blast radius of LLM defects. And now, the Gorilla team from Berkeley AI ([Shishir](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shishir-patil?trk=public_profile__posts-text), [Tianjun](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tianjun-zhang-333bb2126?trk=public_profile__posts-text)) has published a detailed thesis on improving trust in autonomous LLM systems: [https://lnkd.in/e3hrDiRW](https://lnkd.in/e3hrDiRW?trk=public_profile__posts-text) Their approach is called GoEx, and the main takeaway is to teach LLM-Agents the all-powerful "Cmd/Ctrl + Z". Given the original goal and the previously-executed code, the LLM generates new code that can undo the operation. The paper also addresses other risks and how to mitigate them in the GoEx framework. My friend [Cedric](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cedricvidal?trk=public_profile__posts-text) has written an informative blog about this here: [https://lnkd.in/egc7Z8wA](https://lnkd.in/egc7Z8wA?trk=public_profile__posts-text) AI is definitely becoming better at planning and function-calling, but reliability is way more critical when it comes to productionizing these systems. GoEx looks like a promising first step towards designing trust-first agentic frameworks, exciting times ahead.

  Last week, Berkeley AI Research ([Tianjun Zhang](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tianjun-zhang-333bb2126?trk=public_profile__posts-text), [Shishir Patil](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shishir-patil?trk=public_profile__posts-text)) published a new technique called \*Retrieval-Augmented Finetuning\* (RAFT) to adapt pretrained LLMs to specialized domains. They found that this approach outperforms Supervised Finetuning and RAG combined! To recap, RAG is a three-step process:  1. retrieve the query's semantic neighbor documents from a knowledgebase,  2. augment the query with this retrieved context, and 3. generate the pretrained LLM's response to the query and context. In RAFT, the first two steps are identical, but in step 3, it additionally fine-tunes the LLM for better domain-specific performance in RAG. This finetuning uses a synthetic domain dataset with questions, answers, and the query-relevant context. It also includes a "teacher" model's Chain-of-Thought reasoning to help it process information like a larger or "better" model. As a bonus, this also enables the model to adopt the domain's style, tone, and voice - crucial for some fields like healthcare and law. I like this technique for its simplicity and relatability.  The paper compares this approach to how students write open-book exams; reading about the domain and maybe even previous years' Q&A can give the student an advantage, just like RAFT improves how models do RAG in production. [Tianjun](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tianjun-zhang-333bb2126?trk=public_profile__posts-text) and [Shishir](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shishir-patil?trk=public_profile__posts-text) share with me and [Cedric Vidal](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cedricvidal?trk=public_profile__posts-text) why they chose Llama 2 7B for this research project, and how they used Llama on Azure's Model as a Service : [https://lnkd.in/ee2Ef_Sj](https://lnkd.in/ee2Ef_Sj?trk=public_profile__posts-text) Here is the code they use to generate the synthetic dataset: [https://lnkd.in/egG3M-HJ](https://lnkd.in/egG3M-HJ?trk=public_profile__posts-text) Arxiv:  [https://lnkd.in/eYnRSrFr](https://lnkd.in/eYnRSrFr?trk=public_profile__posts-text)

  A newly published paper MaLa-500 ([https://lnkd.in/ezwuMTJw](https://lnkd.in/ezwuMTJw?trk=public_profile__posts-text)) by [Peiqin Lin](https://cn.linkedin.com/in/peiqin-lin-5422a2134?trk=public_profile__posts-text) [Shaoxiong Ji](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/shaoxiongji?trk=public_profile__posts-text) et al has learned Llama2-7B on an impressive 500 different languages. The training approach is straightforward; it involves expanding the vocabulary and training on multilingual datasets using LoRA, which effectively enlarges the model size to 10B. What piques my interest is the fact that they've managed to achieve competitive performance starting from the smallest Llama model. Each language has its unique phrasing and semantic structures - so how did they succeed in learning 500 new languages with just an addition of 2-3 B parameters? Exciting times for multilingual and low-powered AI!

  At the Hinton Lecture in Toronto, Owain Evans presented a study on "subliminal learning", i.e. a model picking up ethical traits from another model through the exchange of data that looks completely neutral. The phenomenon is fascinating, but what interested me most is why it happens. After reading the paper closely, it became clear that the effect comes from a geometric argument linked to the "Earth-Mover" drift between two networks that share the same initialization. To my physics brain, this felt oddly familiar, reminding me of a quantum paradox called superdeterminism, where correlations look “spooky” from the outside but arise naturally from shared initial conditions. There’s an ethical angle here too. Modern AI pipelines rely heavily on models learning from each other, and traits can propagate through structure, not just through text. In my recent work, I explore how AI can realistically support this transition across three converging domains: - Personalized medicine - Longevity & aging science - Critical disease detection and intervention Key ideas include: - Transitioning from population averages to personal baselines built from continuous, multi-modal data (wearables, labs, imaging, genomics). - Dividing AI responsibilities into workflow copilots, risk prediction engines, and causality-driven intervention planning, rather than relying on a single model for all tasks. - Viewing AI not as a black-box recommender, but as a regulated control system that operates under clinical validation, transparency, and governance. - Understanding longevity as early detection of biological drift, safer learning loops, and function-preserving interventions, rather than “biohacking.” - Focusing AI applications where they currently provide the most value: oncology, rare disease diagnosis, sepsis detection, pharmacogenomics, and digital twins.
