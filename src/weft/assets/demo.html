GUIDED WALKTHROUGH

A video becomes a world
an agent can train in.

Reinforcement-learning teams hand-build the environments their agents learn in — engineer-weeks per task, and usually wrong in ways nobody can see until a policy fails. Weft turns an ordinary clip into an executable, inspectable world in about a minute, then has Meta's Muse argue with the result and propose the tests that would catch what it got wrong.

ImpactReplaces engineer-weeks of hand-authored simulation with a one-minute pass over a phone clip.
Meta AIMuse reviews the constructed physics against real evidence and picks the bounded test to run — proposals, never silent edits.
DemoIt runs live, here, on a clip you choose — and every step leaves a bundle you can download.
DepthFrozen gates, preserved failures, append-only spend ledgers. Refusals are shown, not hidden.

What this is, honestly. Worlds built here are conditional models with disclosed assumptions — not verified copies of the physical world. Single person, mostly fixed cameras; multi-person clips are refused with reasons. Muse proposes; it never silently edits a world. Every number on this page came from a run that actually happened on this machine, and failures are preserved rather than quietly retried.
